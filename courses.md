# Course Index

Courses whose public materials we analyze for practices in project-based software engineering teaching with AI tools. One entry per course offering. A course taught in two terms gets two entries if the materials differ.

Field meaning:

- **Term**: when the course was taught. Format: season and year, then months if known.
- **Description**: two or three sentences: level, project format, how AI is treated.
- **Rules and assignments**: links to the syllabus or guide, the AI policy, and the assignment pages that matter for practices.
- **Reflection or analysis**: what the staff or others published about how the course went. "None found" includes the date of the search.
- **Status**: `processed` (a course file exists in `courses/`), `summarized` (covered only in the research reports), `candidate` (found, not yet read).
- **Links checked**: date when the links were last opened. Links from the research reports were not re-opened unless stated.

## MIT — 6.1040 Software Design

- **Term**: Fall 2025 (3 Sep to 10 Dec 2025).
- **Description**: junior and senior undergraduates. Individual project across seven assignments, then the same steps in teams of four. AI encouraged in all work with disclosure in a post-assignment survey; a staff-built tool records every LLM call for grading.
- **Rules and assignments**: [Class Guide](https://61040-fa25.github.io/guide) (includes AI policy), [Assignments list](https://61040-fa25.github.io/assignments), [Assignment 4a: Backend Concept Coding](https://61040-fa25.github.io/assignments/assignment-4a) (workflow, snapshots, interesting moments), [Assignment 4b: Frontend](https://61040-fa25.github.io/assignments/assignment-4b) (agentic tool), [Final Project](https://61040-fa25.github.io/assignments/final-project-main), [FAQ](https://61040-fa25.github.io/faq).
- **Reflection or analysis**: Daniel Jackson, [LLMs are not calculators: Why educators should embrace AI (and fear it)](https://groups.csail.mit.edu/sdg/pubs/2025/jackson-ai-education.pdf), 26 Dec 2025, updated 11 Jan 2026. Personal observations, not a systematic analysis.
- **Status**: processed → [courses/mit-61040-fa25-software_design.md](courses/mit-61040-fa25-software_design.md). Snapshots in [sources/mit-61040-fa25-software_design/](sources/mit-61040-fa25-software_design/).
- **Links checked**: 2026-09-18.

## Stanford — CS146S The Modern Software Developer

- **Term**: Fall 2025 (22 Sep to 10 Dec 2025). First offering.
- **Description**: undergraduate and graduate students, 3 units. Nine weekly individual assignments on staff-provided starter apps, each tied to one AI tool and a guest lecture by that tool's builder, plus an open-ended team project (80% of the grade) with four milestones and a demo day. AI tools are the subject of the course; no course-specific AI policy was found in public materials.
- **Rules and assignments**: [Course site, Fall 2025 archive](https://themodernsoftware.dev/fall2025) (schedule, grading, FAQ; Fall 2025 text preserved in the [Wayback copy of 1 Dec 2025](http://web.archive.org/web/20251201160649/https://themodernsoftware.dev/)), [Assignment deadlines sheet](https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/d/1-485SLHw_zn7A-UXiz88Dgjy_qUGx87Am6HUwQrKsN8/edit?gid=0) (final project milestones), [Assignments repository](https://github.com/mihail911/modern-software-dev-assignments) (weeks 1 to 8 with write-up templates), [Week 4: Autonomous coding agent automations](https://github.com/mihail911/modern-software-dev-assignments/blob/master/week4/assignment.md), [Week 5: Multi-agent workflows](https://github.com/mihail911/modern-software-dev-assignments/blob/master/week5/assignment.md), [Week 6: Scan and fix vulnerabilities](https://github.com/mihail911/modern-software-dev-assignments/blob/master/week6/assignment.md), [Week 7: AI code review](https://github.com/mihail911/modern-software-dev-assignments/blob/master/week7/assignment.md), [Week 8: Multi-stack build](https://github.com/mihail911/modern-software-dev-assignments/blob/master/week8/assignment.md), [Lecture 1 slides](https://docs.google.com/presentation/d/1zT2Ofy88cajLTLkd7TcuSM4BCELvF9qQdHmlz33i4t0/edit) (logistics), [Stanford Bulletin entry](https://bulletin.stanford.edu/courses/2274401). The final project handout and rubrics are on the course learning platform and are not public.
- **Reflection or analysis**: Mihail Eric, [interview in EO magazine](https://www.eomag.io/article/stanford-mihail-eric), 4 Mar 2026. A few remarks on the strongest students and on beginner mistakes with agents; no systematic reflection. No staff retrospective or student data found (2026-09-18).
- **Status**: processed → [courses/stanford-cs146s-fa25-modern_software_developer.md](courses/stanford-cs146s-fa25-modern_software_developer.md). Snapshots in [sources/stanford-cs146s-fa25-modern_software_developer/](sources/stanford-cs146s-fa25-modern_software_developer/).

## Carnegie Mellon — 17-316 / 17-616 AI Tools for Software Development

- **Term**: Fall 2025.
- **Description**: undergraduate and graduate cross-listing, first offering. Teams of 2-3, assigned by staff, imitate an existing startup's product across six two-week sprints (requirements, specification, frontend, backend, testing, deployment) plus a final demo and postmortem. AI use is the subject of the course: individual written reflections must be produced without any AI, while team project deliverables must be generated by an LLM and changed only by re-prompting it, never by hand-editing.
- **Rules and assignments**: [Syllabus](https://ai-developer-tools.github.io/syllabus/) (grading, AI policy, academic honesty), [Overview](https://ai-developer-tools.github.io/overview/), [Teamwork Practices](https://ai-developer-tools.github.io/teamwork/), [Reflection 1 (HW1)](https://ai-developer-tools.github.io/assignments/HW1/) through [Reflection 6 (HW6)](https://ai-developer-tools.github.io/assignments/HW6/), [Project 1: Requirements Engineering](https://ai-developer-tools.github.io/project/p1/) through [Project 7: Final Demo and Postmortem](https://ai-developer-tools.github.io/project/p7/).
- **Reflection or analysis**: Marylee Williams, [CMU Students Put AI Coding Tools to the Test](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/news/2025/ai-tools-for-software), CMU School of Computer Science news, 14 Oct 2025. Instructor and department quotes from the first week of class, not a systematic report. Austin Henley (co-instructor), [Assessing students in the era of AI](https://austinhenley.com/blog/aihomework.html), 10 Aug 2025. Written before the course; a general overview of options other instructors were discussing, not specific to this course. No staff retrospective or outcome data found (2026-09-18).
- **Status**: processed → [courses/cmu-17316-fa25-ai_tools_for_software_development.md](courses/cmu-17316-fa25-ai_tools_for_software_development.md). Snapshots in [sources/cmu-17316-fa25-ai_tools_for_software_development/](sources/cmu-17316-fa25-ai_tools_for_software_development/).
- **Links checked**: 2026-09-18.

## Carnegie Mellon — 15-113 Effective Coding with AI

- **Term**: Spring 2026.
- **Description**: course after introductory programming. Mini-projects and a portfolio; a capstone with an in-person oral check. Includes a code handoff exercise where students inherit another student's unfinished project.
- **Rules and assignments**: [Course archive, Spring 2026](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~mdtaylor/113/S26/), [HW7: Code Handoff](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~mdtaylor/113/S26/hw7.html), [HW8](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~mdtaylor/113/S26/hw8.html) (specify, delegate, review), [Project 3: Capstone](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~mdtaylor/113/S26/project3.html).
- **Reflection or analysis**: Mike Taylor, [Best Practices and Lessons Learned](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~mdtaylor/113/S26/bestPractices.html). Preliminary synthesis of about 320 student comments from nine surveys. The author states most of it was AI-generated and then edited by hand.

## UC San Diego — CSE190 / CSE291P Generative AI and Programming

- **Term**: Spring 2026.
- **Description**: undergraduate and graduate audiences. Several applications including a student-chosen final project. Agentic coding encouraged; peer reviews and interpersonal communication must be human-written. Separate minimum scores for assignments and for reviews; staff may interview a student before releasing a grade.

First-person experience, not student outcomes.
